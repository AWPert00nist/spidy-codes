RentoMojo: Renting a Product Teardown

A feature teardown of the rental journey, from browsing to deposit refund, by Shivam Bajaj. Built only from public sources; I have no access to RentoMojo's internal data.

Overview

RentoMojo, founded in 2014, rents furniture, appliances and home essentials for a monthly fee, so customers avoid the upfront cost of buying. It reported about ₹266 crore revenue in FY25, up roughly 38% from FY24 (Entrackr), and a profit of about ₹40–43 crore depending on the source (YourStory). It has filed a draft prospectus for an IPO (Kotak Neo).

Feature in focus: renting a product. The customer must trust three things: the product will arrive in good shape, the full cost is clear, and the deposit comes back. Each is a product decision as well as an operations one.

Revenue and profit figures come from press coverage of company-supplied numbers and differ between sources. Check them against the filing before reuse.

Customers & Competition

Who rents

  • Young professionals and migrants who move cities often and do not want to buy furniture (ThePrint).
  • Families setting up a home who prefer a monthly cost over a large one-time spend.

Competitors

  • Furlenco, Cityfurnish, Rentickle and GrabOnRent are the usual names (Inc42).
  • One founder says about 90% of the furniture market is still offline, so much of the competition is the decision to buy.

The trust gap

  • Public complaint sites keep returning to deposit refunds, billing clarity and support access (Voxya; ConsumerComplaints).
  • These are self-selected, so they show themes, not rates.

Personas

Illustrative archetypes, not real people. Pain points are linked to a source where one exists; the rest are my hypotheses.

The relocating professional

Who: Moves city for work every year or two

Goals: A furnished home without a big spend or a resale hassle

Pain points: Deposit refund delays and deductions are a recurring complaint (ConsumerComplaints)

Opportunity: Inspection photos and a refund tracker

The first-home family

Who: Setting up a new rented flat on a monthly budget

Goals: Spread cost and swap items as needs change

Pain points: Hypothesis: unclear minimum tenure and early-closure terms

Opportunity: Tenure chips with commitment terms shown clearly

The first-time renter

Who: Never rented furniture and unsure it beats buying

Goals: Confidence that renting is worth it

Pain points: Billing clarity concerns are reported (Trustpilot)

Opportunity: A short rent-versus-buy calculator and one-screen total cost

The short-stay user

Who: On a six-to-twelve month posting or project

Goals: The shortest tenure and an easy early exit

Pain points: Hypothesis: early closure fees are hard to predict

Opportunity: Show early-closure cost before checkout

UX Elements

Illustrative wireframes of proposed screens. They are not screenshots of RentoMojo's app, and all copy and amounts are placeholders.

1. Rent listing: sticky search, ratings, rental count and delivery time on each card

2. Product page: tenure as selectable chips, commitment terms shown after the choice

3. Cart: every charge in one place, plus when the deposit returns

4. Return tracker: pickup, photos, inspection result and refund status

Improvement Ideas

My suggestions, not claims about RentoMojo's roadmap.

1. Richer product cards

Show rating, number of rentals and delivery time on each card, and keep search visible on every listing screen. Why: Helps shoppers who skim lists and tends to improve click-through. Risk: Delivery estimates must be accurate.

2. Clear tenure selection

Show tenures as chips with the monthly price for each, and show minimum commitment and early-closure terms after the choice, not above it. Why: Avoids readers assuming the pre-selected tenure is the only one. Risk: More choices can slow the decision, so keep a default.

3. One-screen total cost

List rent, delivery, deposit and any fees together, with a plain-language note on when the deposit comes back. Why: Billing surprises are a recurring complaint. Risk: Needs the same numbers to appear on the invoice.

4. Refund tracker with inspection photos

Capture photos at pickup, share the inspection result, and let customers dispute a deduction in one tap. Why: Deposit refunds and deductions are the most reported pain point. Risk: Operational cost and disputes to handle.

Key Metrics

North Star

Net new active subscriptions per month.

  • Captures both acquisition and retention of rentals.

L1: Conversion

% of product-page views that become orders.

  • Split by tenure chosen and by listing-card click-through.

L2: Retention

% of subscribers who extend or add another item.

  • Compare customers with a smooth deposit refund and those with a dispute.

Guardrails

  • Days from pickup to deposit refund, and % of deposits with a deduction dispute.
  • Complaints and support contacts per 1,000 orders.
  • Early-closure rate and damage-claim rate, so clearer terms do not hurt unit economics.
