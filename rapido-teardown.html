Rapido: Ride Booking Teardown

Overview

Rapido is an Indian ride-hailing platform that started with bike taxis and now offers autos and cabs. Its pitch to captains (drivers) is a low-cost alternative to the 20–30% cuts typical of the category, using a subscription model for autos and cabs (Outlook Business interview). It was reported to lead rivals with about 16.5 lakh daily rides (Outlook Business).

Feature in focus: booking a ride, from opening the app to a captain being on the way. This is where a rider decides whether the app can be trusted.

Valuation and ride-volume figures vary across sources, so I use only what the cited articles state.

Personas

Illustrative archetypes based on public complaints, not real individuals.

The daily commuter

Who: Office-goer in a metro who books a ride to work every day

Goals: Reach on time at a predictable price

Pain points: Cannot get a ride in peak hours; captains cancel after seeing the drop (ThePrint)

The impromptu rider

Who: Young professional with last-minute plans, often late evening

Goals: Book fast and pay by UPI

Pain points: Unsure whether the captain will cancel; trouble at odd hours or when paying digitally (Business Today)

The budget last-mile rider

Who: Student or shift worker using a bike or auto for short hops

Goals: Lowest fare for a short trip

Pain points: Fare changes or extra money demanded outside the app (Free Press Journal)

The late-night rider

Who: Commuter or student riding home after dark

Goals: Feel safe and reach home quickly

Pain points: Hypothesis: unsure about captain details and trip sharing at night

Opportunity: Prominent trip sharing and captain verification

The captain (supply side)

Who: Bike, auto or cab driver earning per trip

Goals: Steady trips and fair pay

Pain points: Cancellations are a top rider complaint, so the cause matters; destination-visible-first is a reported driver trigger (Business Today)

Opportunity: Fair-fare cues and cancellation feedback to captains

The first-time user

Who: Tries the app after a friend recommends it

Goals: A quick first ride without confusion

Pain points: Hypothesis: unsure which vehicle type to pick and what the fare will be

Opportunity: Simple vehicle guidance and an upfront fare

Cancellations are the recurring theme. LocalCircles surveys put driver cancellations at the top of app-taxi grievances, with the share varying by survey (reported as 79% in one release) (ThePrint; Deccan Herald). These surveys cover app taxis in general, not Rapido alone.

Feature Ideas

My suggestions, not claims about Rapido's roadmap.

1. Priority re-match after a captain cancels

If a captain cancels, automatically re-request the next nearest captain and move the rider up the queue, with no fee to the rider. Why: cancellation is the top complaint. Risk: needs to avoid punishing captains who cancel for genuine reasons.

2. Pickup confidence per ride type

Next to each ride option, show expected pickup time and how likely it is to be accepted soon. Why: helps riders choose the option that actually arrives sooner. Risk: a wrong estimate hurts trust more than none.

3. Book for someone else

Let a rider book for a family member, share live tracking and the captain's details with them. Why: a common need that avoids phone calls. Risk: safety and fraud checks.

4. Add a stop

Allow one or more intermediate stops with the fare updated upfront. Why: fewer off-app negotiations for multi-stop trips. Risk: longer trips are less attractive for captains unless the fare reflects them.

Key Metrics

North Star

Completed rides per week.

  • Counts rides that actually finish, not just bookings, so it rewards reliability.

L1: Fulfilment

% of booking requests that become completed rides.

  • Break down cancellations by reason and by who cancelled.
  • Track time from request to captain accepted.

L2: Retention

% of riders who book again within 30 days.

  • Compare riders who had a cancelled ride with those who did not.

Guardrails

  • Captain-side acceptance rate and earnings per hour, so rider fixes do not hurt supply.
  • Fare disputes and complaints per 1,000 rides.
  • Booking-flow success rate and app availability during peaks.
